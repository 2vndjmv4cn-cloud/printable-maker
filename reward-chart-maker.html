Reward Chart Maker

Make a printable reward chart. Choose the goal, the number of stickers and the prize, then print. Great for potty training, reading and good habits.

How to use it

  1. Write one clear goal in your child's words, like "I stay in my bed all night" or "I use the potty".
  2. Choose how many stickers it takes to earn the reward. Younger kids do best with 5 to 10; older kids can work toward 20 or 30.
  3. Print it, put it somewhere they can see it, and add a sticker (or a colored-in shape) each time the goal happens.

Making a reward chart work

  • One goal at a time. A single behavior is easier to notice and praise than a list.
  • Reward soon. For little kids, a first chart they can finish within a week keeps it exciting.
  • Praise as you go. The sticker matters, but the attention matters more.
  • Never remove stickers. Earned is earned. Charts work best as a positive tool.

Questions

What are good rewards?

Experiences often work better than toys: a trip to the park, choosing dinner, a later bedtime on Saturday or a movie night.

Do I need sticker paper?

No. Kids can color in each shape with a crayon or marker instead.

Can I make one for an adult goal?

Yes. Use it for workouts, no-spend days or anything you want to count.
